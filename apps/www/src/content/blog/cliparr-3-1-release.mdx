**Cliparr 3.1 brings a new mobile editing experience.** We've cleared out clutter, given the preview more space, and moved the tools into a bottom action bar that keeps everything within reach. Pick a moment, adjust the framing, add subtitles, and export, all from your phone's browser.

And there's a new **Crop tool** for videos, GIFs, and screenshots. You can finally choose both _when_ your clip happens and _what_ fits in the frame.

## A full edit from your phone

Here's the new mobile experience in action: trim the moment, crop the picture, style the subtitles, and export the finished clip. The demo uses the subtitle track provided by the media server, so we can quickly drop in the official dialogue text with its existing timing instead of typing it out ourselves.

## More room for the edit

Cliparr has always worked on a phone, but the old layout carried a lot of desktop baggage: crowded controls, excess padding, and a preview fighting for space. The new layout puts the picture first, with controls arranged for a smaller screen.

The bottom action bar groups your tools into **Trim, Subtitles, Crop, Playback, and More**. Open the controls you need for the current step, then get back to your preview and timeline.

Cliparr runs in your browser, using WebCodecs and Mediabunny for editing and export. There's no app to install. If you haven't tried it on your phone yet, this is a good time to give it a go.

## Crop your clips

But wait! There's more! <InlineImage src={billyMays} alt="Billy Mays giving a thumbs-up" />

Cliparr can now clip through _time and space_, with the new <span aria-hidden="true" className={buttonClassName({ tone: "icon", size: "icon", className: "align-middle rounded-[var(--radius-control)]" })}><Crop className="h-4 w-4" /></span> Crop tool. Before, you could trim the beginning or end of a clip. Now you can clip… the clip! (Did I go too far?)

Draw a freeform rectangle over the preview, choose an aspect-ratio preset, or enter exact pixel values. Pull a vertical clip out of a wider shot, remove an unwanted edge, or focus on the part of the picture you want to share. The same crop applies to video, GIFs, and screenshots.

On a phone, tap **Crop** in the bottom action bar. Drag the selection on the preview or use the numeric fields below it, then tap **Done** to review the result with subtitles. Small selections hide the side handles so the corner handles stay easy to reach. On desktop, crop settings sit alongside subtitles in the expandable Properties panels.

Cropping supports **undo and redo**, saves with your draft, and includes keyboard shortcuts for precise adjustments. See the [cropping guide](/docs/video-cropping) for the details.

## Bring the dialogue with you

The subtitle tools are part of the mobile workflow too. When Plex or Jellyfin provides a supported text subtitle track, you can use its dialogue and timing as your starting point, then adjust the styling and position for your clip. You can also edit the text or write your own captions.

Subtitles are positioned within the cropped picture and burned into the exported video or GIF, so they stay with the clip when you share it. See [subtitle burn-in](/docs/subtitle-burn-in) for supported tracks and editing options.

## A few other fixes

There's more in 3.1 besides the two big feature changes:

- **Clearer playback loading.** Each source shows whether it is connecting, finding playback, or preparing playback details. Ready sources remain available while others continue loading.
- **Faster Plex connection attempts.** Cliparr tries server addresses in a staggered sequence, reducing the wait when an earlier address is slow or unavailable.
- **A warning before leaving an active export.** Navigating away from the editor asks for confirmation. Reloading or closing the tab also requests a browser warning.
- **FLAC export fixes.** Valid standalone FLAC files with short final frames export in full. Incomplete sources are still rejected instead of producing silently truncated audio.
- **Timeline fixes.** We've fixed playback and scrubbing issues at clip boundaries and when the browser suspends an audio context.

## Faster builds and security updates

For anyone building Cliparr from source, Docker now compiles the app on the builder's native architecture. Producing an ARM64 image no longer needs to emulate dependency installation and frontend/server compilation. CI builds and smoke-tests both AMD64 and ARM64 production images.

We've also updated dependencies and container images, including the Node base image with the APT security fix. The container security scanner is updated too, and the deployment examples use stronger defaults.
